🎧 Mr. Mic

Turn a headset on, plug earphones in — Windows just follows.

What it does: switches the Windows default speaker AND microphone together between as many devices as you like — HyperX headset, wired earphones, Bluetooth headphones, laptop speakers — including the communications device Discord uses. Shows headset battery. Mutes your speakers or mic from the tray. Has a built-in per-app mixer.

Whatever you just turned on wins. Plug in earphones while the headset is on and you get the earphones — switching something on is treated as you asking for it. The list order in Settings only decides where to land when the thing you were using disappears.

Muting

  • Double-click the tray icon to mute your microphone, and again to unmute. For when someone walks in and starts talking.
  • Right-click → 🔇 Mute speakers or 🎤 Mute microphone. These mute the device itself, so every app is silenced at once — a real hardware-style mute, not a per-app one.
  • Give either one a hotkey in Settings. Worth doing: in a full-screen game the tray isn't reachable, and a key under your hand beats hunting for a 16-pixel icon.
  • The tray icon turns red while your speakers are muted.
  • For one app only, use the mixer instead — click the app's icon.
Why single-click feels a beat slower now. Windows can't tell a single click from the first half of a double click until its double-click time (half a second) has passed, so switching by click waits that long to be sure. If you'd rather have instant clicks than a double-click mute, set Double-click the tray icon to Off in Settings — the right-click menu and the hotkey still mute.

Bluetooth — handing headphones to your phone

Right-click → 📶 Bluetooth lists everything paired. A tick means connected. Click a ticked one to disconnect it, an unticked one to connect it.

Why not just turn Bluetooth off? Because your headphones aren't the only thing on it — four serial-over-Bluetooth COM ports go down with the radio. Disconnecting the one device leaves everything else alone.

The usual reason to do this: your headphones connect to whichever paired device answers first, and a phone in your pocket usually beats a laptop. Disconnect here, and the phone gets them. Click again later and Mr. Mic switches back to them automatically.

Give it a second or two — Bluetooth is not instant. You get a notification when it's done rather than the menu freezing.

The mixer (middle-click)

  • Each output device: name, master slider, 🔊 mute button on the right.
  • Each app under it: icon + slider. Click the icon to mute/unmute that app (icon dims when muted). Hover the icon to see the app's name.
  • 🎤 rows at the bottom: your microphones' input levels — turn the laptop mic down here if a dictation app picks up room noise.
  • The ● marks the current default device. Esc or click away to close.

Settings — right-click → ⚙ Settings…

Everything is in one window. No text editor, no Visual Studio.

Hotkeys

Click a box, press the keys you want, and it's set. ✕ clear next to it puts it back to no hotkey at all. Three of them: switch device, mute speakers, mute microphone. Anything works — ctrl+alt+h, a single pause key, v+c (two keys held together), shift+ctrl+p.

Setting up a device that isn't here right now — a Bluetooth headset you left at home, say. The dropdown lists everything Windows has ever seen, marked NotPresent. Pair it once, then pick it from that list any time afterwards and it'll be ready when you next turn it on.
Bluetooth headphones: leave the microphone on “— leave alone —”. Bluetooth has two modes: Headphones (…) is proper stereo, Headset (…) turns the mic on but drops the sound to tinny phone-call quality. Mr. Mic points at the good one and leaves your mic where it was. Only pick the Headset (…) mic if you actually want to talk through those headphones, and expect the music to sound bad while you do.

Tips & troubleshooting

  • Discord: set its input/output to Default in Voice settings once — then it always follows Mr. Mic.
  • A device shows “(off)” and is greyed out: Windows can't see it. Turn it on, plug it in, or connect it in Bluetooth settings.
  • Something switched and you didn't want it to: untick auto for that device in Settings (Edit → uncheck "switch to it automatically"). It stays in the menu for switching by hand.
  • Dictation going crazy on switch? Pin it to a specific mic in its own settings, or lower that mic in the mixer.
  • Battery shows “—” (headset off?): the HyperX is switched off. That's also how Mr. Mic knows — the dongle only answers while the headset is on.
  • “🔌 Dongle unplugged”: different problem. The little USB receiver isn't in a port, so turning the headset on does nothing — there's nothing listening. Plug it back in, or pull it out and reseat it if it's already there (a USB port that slept and never woke up looks identical to an empty one).
  • App not running? Double-click MrMic.exe (or run-mrmic.bat). It also autostarts with Windows. A second copy won't start twice — that's intentional.
  • Gaming: Mr. Mic runs at below-normal priority, ~0.05% CPU, no GPU — it cannot affect DCS/VR performance.
  • Logs: mrmic.log next to the app records every switch.
